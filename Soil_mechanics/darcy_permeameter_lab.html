Governing Formula: Falling Head Permeability ASTM D2434 Standard
K_T = (a · L / A · t) · ln(h₁ / h₂)

Where a is standpipe area, A is permeameter area, L is length, and t is time required for hydraulic head to drop from h₁ down to h₂.

🧪 Constant Head Permeameter (ASTM D2434)

Used exclusively for coarse-grained soils (gravels and sands where K > 10⁻⁴ cm/s). Water flows rapidly enough that an inflow reservoir with an overflow weir maintains a constant hydraulic head (Δh), while effluent water is gathered in a graduated beaker.

⏱️ Falling Head Permeameter (ASTM D5084)

Used for fine-grained soils (silts and clays where K < 10⁻⁴ cm/s). Flow through clay is too slow to measure in a beaker. Instead, water is supplied via a narrow standpipe (a). We measure the elapsed time t required for head to drop from h₁ to h₂: K = (a·L / A·t) · ln(h₁ / h₂).

Using Custom Soil Laboratory Data

1. Set Geometry: Adjust the Specimen Length (L) and Diameter (D) sliders on the left to match your permeameter mould dimensions.

2. Set Temperature: Adjust the water temperature slider to your lab thermometer reading. The tool automatically applies the viscosity correction factor RT = μ_T / μ_20°C to report standard K₂₀°C.

3. Enter Readings: For Constant Head, input your measured volume in mL and time in seconds. For Falling Head, input your initial head h₁, final head h₂, and elapsed seconds.

4. The tool instantly solves for K, checks whether Darcy's linear laminar regime holds (Re < 1), and classifies your soil according to standard geotechnical criteria.
