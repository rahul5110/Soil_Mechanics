Governing Relationships: Soil Compaction & Zero Air Voids (ZAV) Soil Mechanics Formulation
Dry Density: ρ_d = ρ / (1 + w)
Zero Air Voids: ρ_{zav} = (G_s · ρ_w) / (1 + w · G_s)

Why the compaction curve drops wet of optimum: Beyond the Optimum Moisture Content (OMC), added water fills the interstitial void spaces between soil grains. Because water is incompressible under dynamic rammer blows and traps residual air bubbles, it forces soil particles apart, preventing further reduction in void ratio and causing dry density to decline parallel to the Zero Air Voids (ZAV) line. The curve can never cross or reach the 100% ZAV line in dynamic field or laboratory compaction because it is physically impossible to expel 100% of entrapped air bubbles by mechanical rolling or tamping alone!

🚜 Standard Proctor (ASTM D698 / IS 2720-VII)

Designed to simulate historical light compaction equipment. Employs a 2.60 kg (5.5 lb) rammer falling through 310 mm (12 in) onto 3 soil layers, imparting a total nominal compactive energy of approximately 594 kJ/m³. Typical for earthen dams, agricultural embankments, and light subgrade fills.

✈️ Modified Proctor (ASTM D1557 / IS 2720-VIII)

Developed during World War II for heavy aircraft runways and modern highway traffic.
